I FINALLY MADE A TURING COMPLETE MACHINE IN TURING COMPLETE!
[Turing Complete](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1444480/Turing_Complete/) is this awesome game you
should definitely try if you ever get the chance. You basically just learn CPU architecture through puzzles
and it's a really cool intro into computer logic.

I started out failing on super basic stuff. Even my NOT gate level took me a while, if you couldn't tell
from the extra floating NAND for no reason.

![My NOT gate solution, with an extra NAND gate sitting there doing nothing](/images/turing-complete-not-gate.jpg)

BUT learning how to make all the components in pieces was super cool, and if it wasn't for the gamified and
broken down approach to making a Turing complete machine I don't think I ever would have gotten there.

![My first ALU, built out of NAND and NOT gates](/images/turing-complete-alu.jpg)

It was 50 hours of amazing ups and downs, hitting my desk in rage, having five sheets of paper for the same
damn problem, and seeing that onwards image giving me hope after each level.

![The finished machine: registers, ALU, counter, and program memory all wired together](/images/turing-complete-cpu.jpg)

I only completed the first half of the game, and I think that is where I will stop. I now have a basic
foundation to begin full diving into Verilog and FPGAs. I am planning to really lock in on that stuff now
that midterm hell is over, because the MiSTer project seems so cool.

![The end screen: "You did it! I thought you were just this odd looking hairless ape creature, but you actually built a real computer! Amazing."](/images/turing-complete-win.jpg)

Anyway I am just so happy and sad at the same time to know my CompE journey has just started but Turing
Complete for me is over. I am going to Wawa to celebrate!
